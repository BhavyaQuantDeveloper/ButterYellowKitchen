The Art of Wanting Everything: On Pursuing All Your Interests at Once

February 24, 2026
Dear woman with too many tabs open,

You have a half-finished watercolour on your desk, a stack of philosophy books on your nightstand, a sourdough starter that needs feeding, three saved playlists for three different moods, a business idea in your notes app, and a sudden, burning desire to learn Portuguese.

People have told you to pick one thing. To go deep. To specialise. They say it with concern in their voices, as if wanting too much is a symptom of something broken.

It is not. It is a symptom of being alive.

The myth of the single calling

Somewhere along the way, we were sold the idea that every person has one purpose, one vocation, one lane — and that finding it requires eliminating everything else. This works beautifully for some people. And it is suffocating for the rest of us.

If you have ever felt guilty for starting something new before finishing something old, if you have ever whispered but I also want to… and then stopped yourself — this letter is for you.

The woman with many interests is not scattered. She is vast. And the world has never known what to do with vast women.

What pursuing everything actually looks like

Let me be honest. Pursuing all your interests does not mean doing all of them at the same time, at the same intensity, forever. It means giving yourself permission to rotate. To have seasons. To be obsessed with pottery for three months and then let it rest while you fall into astrophysics.

The key is not discipline. It is devotion without guilt.

The Rotation Principle

Keep three lanes open. At any given time, have one interest that is active (the thing you are currently obsessed with), one that is simmering (you think about it, you read about it, but you are not actively doing it), and one that is resting (it will come back when it is ready). This is not abandonment. This is rhythm.

 

Lower the entry bar. You do not need to enrol in a course to learn something. You do not need the perfect supplies to start. Read one chapter. Watch one video. Sketch one bad drawing. The woman who begins imperfectly begins a thousand times more often than the woman who waits to be ready.

 

Let interests cross-pollinate. The most interesting ideas live at the intersection of things. Your love of cooking and your love of chemistry are not separate — fermentation is both. Your interest in philosophy and your interest in skincare are not unrelated — both are about how we care for something fragile. When your interests talk to each other, you stop being a dilettante and become a thinker.

On the fear of being a beginner

Here is a secret that nobody tells you: being a beginner is the most creative state you will ever be in. Before you know the rules, before you know what is correct, you are free. You make connections that experts miss. You ask questions that open doors.

The woman who is willing to be bad at something new will always outgrow the woman who only does what she is already good at.

The interests you cannot explain

Some of your interests will make sense to other people. Cooking. Reading. Fitness. These are legible. People understand them.

But then there are the other ones. The inexplicable fascination with maritime history. The three-hour rabbit hole about type design. The sudden need to understand how bridges are built. These are the interests that make you you. They are not frivolous. They are the texture of a rich inner life.

Never apologise for what makes you curious. Curiosity is not a flaw. It is a compass.

A note on depth

People will say: but you will never be an expert at anything if you spread yourself thin.

This assumes that expertise is the only valuable relationship you can have with a subject. It is not. You can love something without mastering it. You can know enough about wine to enjoy it deeply without becoming a sommelier. You can play piano badly and still have it be the best part of your Tuesday evening.

The goal is not mastery of everything. The goal is a life so textured and full that when you sit down at the end of the day, you feel like you lived.

She did not choose one thing. She chose herself — and herself was many things.

What I want you to take from this

Keep the watercolour on your desk. Feed the sourdough. Open Duolingo for Portuguese. Read the philosophy and the thriller and the cookbook. Start the business idea even if you abandon it. Especially if you abandon it — because you will take something from it into the next thing, and the next thing, and the next.

You are not too much. You are not unfocused. You are a woman who is interested in being alive, and that is the most beautiful thing I can think of.

With a hundred open tabs and no regrets,
