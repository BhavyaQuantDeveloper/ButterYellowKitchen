The Indian Skincare Edit: What Actually Works, From Someone Who Has Tried It All

February 24, 2026
Dear woman standing in the skincare aisle at Nykaa,

I know what you are doing. You are reading the back of a bottle with a thirty-seven-ingredient list, wondering whether niacinamide and vitamin C can go together, whether this sunscreen will leave a white cast on Indian skin, and whether the Instagram dermatologist who told you to use retinol at twenty-three was right or just sponsored.

I have been you. I have spent embarrassing amounts of time and money figuring out what actually works for Indian skin — in Indian weather, at Indian price points, with Indian water. This is everything I wish someone had told me.

First, let us talk about what Indian skin actually needs

Most mainstream skincare advice comes from Western contexts — temperate climates, hard water, lighter skin tones. Indian skin has its own reality: higher melanin (which means more hyperpigmentation but also more natural sun protection), humidity that swings between desert-dry winters and monsoon-drenched summers, and water quality that varies wildly between cities.

The single biggest mistake Indian women make is building a routine designed for someone else's skin in someone else's climate.

The best skincare routine for you is the one that accounts for your city, your water, your skin, and your life — not the one that went viral on someone else's face.

The cleanser: where most routines go wrong

If your face feels tight and squeaky after washing, your cleanser is too harsh. That clean feeling is your skin screaming. Indian pharmacies are full of cheap, alkaline face washes that strip your barrier and leave you producing more oil to compensate.

What actually works

Cleanse Me by Simple. The micellar gel wash. Gentle, no fragrance, rinses clean without stripping. Available everywhere and costs under 400 rupees. I have tried cleansers five times the price and keep coming back.

Minimalist 2% Salicylic Acid Cleanser. For the days your skin feels congested or oily. Not for daily use — twice a week, maximum. It unclogs without over-drying, which is rare for an SA cleanser at this price point (around 350 rupees).

For dry skin: Cetaphil Gentle Skin Cleanser. Boring. Unglamorous. Endlessly effective. The dermatologists recommend it for a reason. It will never be trendy and it will never let you down.

The serum question: niacinamide is your best friend

If I had to pick one active ingredient for Indian skin, it would be niacinamide. Not retinol, not vitamin C, not glycolic acid. Niacinamide. It controls oil, fades dark spots, strengthens the skin barrier, and plays well with everything. It was made for us.

The serums worth your money

Minimalist 10% Niacinamide. The one that started the Indian skincare revolution. Effective, no-nonsense, transparent about what is inside. Around 350 rupees. Use it morning and night under your moisturiser.

Dot & Key Vitamin C Serum (for mornings). If you want brightness — not whitening, brightness — this one delivers. Indian skin tends to look dull in pollution-heavy cities, and a stable vitamin C helps. Layer it under sunscreen. Closer to 700 rupees, but a bottle lasts two months.

Minimalist 0.3% Retinol (for nights, once you are ready). Start slow. Once a week, then twice, then every other night. Never without moisturiser on top. Retinol is the gold standard for texture and fine lines, but Indian skin — especially darker tones — can hyperpigment if you go too fast. Patience. Always patience.

Sunscreen: the one non-negotiable

I know. You have heard this. But I need to say it again because most Indian women are still not wearing sunscreen daily, or they are wearing the wrong kind. The wrong kind is the thick, white, chalky sunscreen your mother put on you at the beach. That is not what we are talking about.

Sunscreens that do not feel like punishment

Re'equil Ultra Matte Dry Touch SPF 50. This is the one. If you have oily or combination skin and you live in a humid Indian city, this sunscreen will change your relationship with sun protection. No white cast, no greasiness, sits beautifully under makeup. Around 700 rupees. I have repurchased it more times than I can count.

Minimalist SPF 50 Sunscreen (for dry skin). More hydrating, slightly dewier finish. If Re'equil feels too matte for you in winter, this is the alternative. No white cast on medium to deep Indian skin tones.

La Shield Fisico SPF 50 (mineral option). If your skin is sensitive or reactive and chemical sunscreens sting, this is the gentlest mineral option available in India. It does leave a slight cast on deeper skin tones — I will not lie to you — but it settles after ten minutes. Around 600 rupees.

Moisturiser: less is more

Indian women in humid climates often skip moisturiser because their skin feels oily. This is a mistake. Dehydrated skin overproduces oil to compensate. Even oily skin needs hydration — just the right kind.

The moisturisers I trust

Neutrogena Hydro Boost Water Gel. Lightweight, sinks in instantly, no residue. Perfect for oily and combination skin in Indian summers. Around 500 rupees. It feels like water and your skin drinks it.

Cetaphil DAM (Daily Advance Moisturising Lotion). For dry skin or North Indian winters when the air turns brutal. Rich without being greasy. A pharmacy staple. Under 400 rupees.

Minimalist Sepicalm 3% + Oat Moisturiser. If your skin is irritated, red, or recovering from over-exfoliating (we have all been there), this calms everything down. Fragrance-free, barrier-repairing, under 400 rupees.

The things nobody talks about

Your water matters. If you live in a city with hard water — Delhi, Bangalore, most of North India — it is silently ruining your skin. A basic tap water filter is a better investment than any serum. The residue from hard water sits on your skin and causes dullness, dryness, and breakouts that no product can fix.

Fragrance is not your friend. Most Indian skincare brands load their products with fragrance because we associate nice-smelling products with quality. But fragrance is one of the most common irritants. If your skin is reactive, start by cutting fragrance from everything that stays on your face (moisturiser, serum, sunscreen). You can keep it in your body lotion.

The 200-rupee product can be better than the 2000-rupee one. Indian pharmacy brands — Cetaphil, CeraVe (now available), Vaseline Intensive Care — have decades of dermatological research behind them. Do not dismiss them because they are not aesthetic. Effective does not need to be pretty.

A complete routine, start to finish

Morning

1. Rinse with water (or gentle cleanser if oily).
2. Niacinamide serum or Vitamin C serum.
3. Lightweight moisturiser.
4. Sunscreen. Always. Even if you are staying home near a window.

Night

1. Oil cleanse or micellar water (if wearing makeup or sunscreen).
2. Gentle cleanser.
3. Treatment serum (niacinamide, or retinol 1–2x a week).
4. Moisturiser. Slightly richer than your morning one.

That is it. Four steps, morning and night. No twelve-step routine. No essence-toner-ampoule-serum-emulsion confusion. Just cleanse, treat, hydrate, protect. Everything else is optional.

The deeper truth

Skincare in India is complicated by colourism, by marketing that sells fairness as beauty, by brands that prey on insecurity. I want to be clear about something: the goal of skincare is healthy skin. Not lighter skin, not flawless skin, not someone else's skin. Your skin, at its healthiest.

When your skin is hydrated, protected, and cared for, it glows. Not because of a filter, not because of a shade, but because health is visible. That glow belongs to women of every skin tone. It always has.
